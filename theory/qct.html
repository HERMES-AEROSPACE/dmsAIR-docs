Quasi-Classical Trajectories

Each collision in dmsAIR is resolved by a full quasi-classical trajectory (QCT) integration on the selected potential energy surface. The trajectory machinery is inherited from CoarseAIR; dmsAIR adds the driver layer that sets up the approach geometry, invokes the integrator, and extracts the post-collision arrangement.

Hamiltonian formulation

For an \(N\)-atom collision system, the dynamical state is the set of atomic positions and momenta \(\{q_i, p_i\}_{i=1..N}\). The non-relativistic Hamiltonian is

\[H = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \frac{p_i^2}{2 m_i} + V(q_1, \ldots, q_N) ,\]

where \(V\) is the ab initio PES. The numerical integration of this Hamiltonian follows the standard molecular-dynamics machinery reviewed in [Frenkel]. CoarseAIR works internally in Jacobi coordinates for the three-body case and in atom-centred Cartesian coordinates for four-body. The PES is evaluated per step by CoarseAIR’s potcl routine, which returns both \(V\) and the atomic forces \(F_i = -\partial V / \partial q_i\). dmsAIR itself does not touch the PES — it treats it as an opaque oracle.

Initial-state sampling

For a three-body event A + BC, the diatom’s rovibrational state \((v, j)\) is drawn from the bath’s current population. Two strategies coexist in the literature [GroverSchwartzentruber2019] and dmsAIR implements the most accurate one: strategy (c), in which the molecule carries a persistent \((q, \dot q)\) between collisions and only the orientation is re-randomised before each new event. This choice avoids the rapid energy drift introduced by re-quantising coordinates at every collision.

The projectile atom is placed at a separation \(D_{\mathrm{init}}\) along an axis offset by the impact parameter \(b\) from the diatom’s centre of mass. The relative-velocity magnitude is fixed by the NTC-drawn translational energy,

\[g_{\mathrm{rel}} = \sqrt{2 E_{\mathrm{rel}} / \mu} ,\]

where \(\mu\) is the reduced mass of the colliding partners. For four-body collisions (AB + CD) the projectile diatom is generated from its own \((v_2, j_2)\) and placed symmetrically with respect to the target.

Integration and termination

Hamilton’s equations

\[\dot q_i = \frac{\partial H}{\partial p_i} , \qquad \dot p_i = -\frac{\partial H}{\partial q_i} ,\]

are integrated from \(t = 0\) until convergence. A trajectory is declared converged when any atom-pair separation exceeds \(R_{\max}\) (meaning at least one product has escaped) or the wall-time exceeds \(t_{\max}\). Trajectories reaching \(t_{\max}\) without separating correspond to long-lived complexes; they are flagged as non-converged and excluded from the rate-coefficient statistics.

See Trajectory Integrators for the available Bulirsch–Stoer and Velocity-Verlet integrators.

Arrangement classification

At convergence, dmsAIR inspects the final inter-atomic distances and identifies the pair with the smallest \(r\) (or the set of such pairs for four-body). This yields the arrangement code used downstream; see Reactive Channels.

Semiclassical quantisation

At convergence, the surviving diatomic product is characterised by its relative position \(\mathbf{r}\) and conjugate momentum \(\mathbf{p}\). Its internal (vibrational + rotational) energy is

\[E = \frac{p_r^2}{2 \mu} + \frac{L^2}{2 \mu r^2} + V(r) ,\]

where \(p_r\) is the radial momentum, \(L = |\mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{p}|\) is the magnitude of the rotational angular momentum, and \(V(r)\) is the diatomic potential associated with the surviving pair.

The product’s coordinates are passed to CoarseAIR’s FindState routine, which recovers the semiclassical quantum numbers \((v', j')\) by inverting the Bohr–Sommerfeld quantisation conditions:

Rotational quantum number. The conserved classical angular momentum is mapped onto an integer \(j'\) via

\[j' + \tfrac{1}{2} = \frac{L}{\hbar} ,\]

so that \(j'(j'+1) \hbar^2 \to L^2\) in the correspondence limit. Equivalently, \(j' = \lfloor L/\hbar - \tfrac{1}{2} \rfloor\) rounded to the nearest non-negative integer. Because \(L\) is constant along the asymptotic product trajectory, evaluating it at the end of the integration is unambiguous.

Vibrational quantum number. Once \(j'\) is fixed, \(v'\) is obtained from the radial action integral

\[v' + \tfrac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{\pi \hbar} \oint p_r \, dr = \frac{1}{\pi \hbar} \oint \sqrt{ 2 \mu \bigl( E - V_{\mathrm{eff}}(r) \bigr) } \; dr ,\]

evaluated between the inner and outer classical turning points of the effective radial potential

\[V_{\mathrm{eff}}(r) = V(r) + \frac{\hbar^2 j'(j'+1)}{2 \mu r^2} .\]

The turning points are located numerically by CoarseAIR’s TurningPoint routine, and the action integral is evaluated by Gauss-Legendre quadrature with nquad nodes (a run-time input). The final \((v', j')\) is then rounded to the nearest valid bound state and matched against the levels database to recover the quantised internal energy \(\varepsilon_{v' j'}\).

For heteronuclear exchange in a polyatomic system (e.g. NO + C → CN + O on the 4A2 surface), the product molecule must be quantised against its own diatomic potential — the quantum numbers are obtained from the CN (or CO) potential, not the original NO potential. dmsAIR selects the correct pair’s potential (MolState%iPair = max(arrangement, 1)) before calling FindState.

Energy-conservation diagnostic

The drift \(|\Delta H| = |H(t_{\mathrm{end}}) - H(0)|\) is recorded for every converged trajectory. Following the SPARTA-QCT convention adopted in [Torres2024], all converged trajectories are accepted in the statistics regardless of \(|\Delta H|\); the drift is reported as a diagnostic only.
